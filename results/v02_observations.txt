  - In 4 of 6 transfer cells, memory from another domain SIGNIFICANTLY INCREASES adaptation cost and evaluations-to-target, with quality unchanged:
    memory-ranked candidates are evaluated before the cheap synthesized candidate that the cold agent tries first, and that cheap one already meets the target.
  - Principle machinery works end to end (extraction, cross-domain instantiation, testing) but tests are mostly CONTRADICTED: the extracted
    principles do not predict what works under new conditions in these domains.
  - Economics: DO_NOT_ADAPT occurs in Domain A (target met early); always-adapt burns more evaluations without quality gain; never-adapt is cheap
    but cannot recover when conditions change.
  - Final architectures are mostly at zero structural distance from the first choice: cheapest-first search finds the useful region immediately.
    This is a ceiling effect of the synthetic domains and the main threat to the informativeness of the transfer test.
  - Causes to consider for v0.3: harder domains where the cheapest architecture is NOT in the top decile; richer principle extraction;
    principle priors weighted by test history (currently untested principles get a prior that outranks cheap unknowns).
